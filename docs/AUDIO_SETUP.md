# Supply human voice and licensed music

The code and subtitles are implemented. **No actual voice, music or effect recordings are supplied.** Empty IDs are intentional. There is no synthetic speech fallback. The geometric presenter is an original static character, not a lip-synced or motion-captured actor.

## 1. Obtain appropriate recordings

Record yourself, commission a consenting voice actor with permission for this Roblox experience, or obtain an appropriate license. Keep proof of consent/license. Do not impersonate a recognizable person or extract another game's soundtrack/voice. A recording being publicly accessible does not establish permission to use it.

Record 60 separate number clips, with the spoken number beginning immediately; avoid music or long leading silence. Record these eight additional short lines (the exact subtitles are editable in the manifest):

| Manifest key | Current subtitle/script |
| --- | --- |
| `upcoming` | Your next round begins in fifteen seconds. |
| `start` | Let's begin! Watch for your numbers. |
| `lastCall` | All twenty numbers are called. Finish your marks. |
| `results` | Round complete. Here are your results. |
| `coin` | Coin flip is beginning! |
| `duck` | Duck hunt is beginning! |
| `pacinki` | Pačinki is beginning! |
| `crazy` | Go CRazy is beginning! |

Number keys are `number_1` … `number_60`; their subtitles are “Number 1.” … “Number 60.” This is **68 individual voice slots** in total. Ask the creator/actor for the intended pronunciation of Pačinki rather than substituting another name.

Keep each voice clip at most **3 seconds** for the current four-second ball cadence. Trim silence, measure its full duration, and put that duration in the manifest. Clips are stopped when their declared slot ends; entering a duration shorter than the recording will truncate it. Change the timing architecture deliberately if longer narration is needed—do not simply exceed this guard.

## 2. Upload and grant experience access

Use Roblox's audio import/upload workflow in Studio or Creator Dashboard under the account/group that owns the test experience. Wait for processing/moderation, record the actual audio asset IDs, and grant the experience permission to use restricted audio through the asset's permissions settings where required. Test in a separate private experience owned by the correct account/group.

Do not paste authentication tokens into source or chat. Numeric audio asset IDs are not account secrets.

## 3. Fill the manifest

Edit `src/shared/AudioManifest.luau`, or in Studio `ReplicatedStorage.Cloudpop.AudioManifest`.

Each entry has:

- `assetId`: the actual recording's `rbxassetid://` URI, replacing the empty string.
- `duration`: measured playback duration in seconds, greater than zero and at most three.
- `text`: the matching subtitle, including the correct called number.

The number entries are generated by the loop at the end of the module. Add assignments **after that loop and before `return manifest`** to set each `manifest["number_" .. number].assetId` and measured `.duration`, or replace the loop with a complete literal table. Keep all 60 keys. Never renumber recordings by array order if their spoken content differs.

No placeholder numeric asset IDs are provided in this repository. The test suite's synthetic IDs are mock-only and must not be copied into the manifest.

The server puts one unique cue ID/key/text/timestamp in the same state update as the ball reveal. Clients preload configured assets. Loaded recordings arriving within 0.5 seconds of the cue begin with that reveal; slower arrivals, missing assets and late joins use the current subtitle instead of playing stale numbers. This avoids a voice saying a previous ball while the next ball is displayed. Real network/asset timing still needs the Studio/client test below.

## 4. Fill continuous music and effects

Edit `Config.Audio.Music` and `Config.Audio.SFX` in `src/shared/Config.luau`:

| Slot | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| `Music.lobby` | Cohesive looping lobby/main-round soundtrack |
| `Music.bonusIntro` | Nonlooping introduction before the active bonus track |
| `Music.coin` | Bright, rhythmic flip-and-reveal theme |
| `Music.duck` | Playful plucked shuffle/choice theme |
| `Music.pacinki` | Cascading mallet/drop theme |
| `Music.crazy` | Energetic wheel/choice theme |
| `SFX.draw`, `mark`, `qualify`, `reveal`, `result` | Short original/licensed feedback cues |

Use actual permitted `rbxassetid://` IDs. Music loops except for `bonusIntro`. Missing bonus/intro assets stay silent; the lobby track is not substituted. Music transitions use one Sound with sequential fades; there are no unrelated simultaneous tracks. During audible voice, music is reduced to 22% of the selected volume, then restored. Four reusable Sounds bound overlapping effects. Voice, music and effects each have independent volume and mute in Settings.

Scene music routing uses the intro slot first, then the corresponding bonus track, returning to lobby only after the queue is clear. Production bonus starts and bonus-name voice integration remain unfinished. Individual qualification currently produces a private visual notice and optional qualification effect; it does not start a shared bonus soundtrack or voice announcement.

The requested references are preserved in `Config.Audio.References`: lobby/main `https://youtu.be/8bTuNaVfNTE`, bonus intro `https://youtu.be/dzdTQrzWS_M`. These are not playable Roblox assets. No download, reupload, replacement track or rights verification has occurred. Obtain permission and supply permitted Roblox audio IDs. `BonusIntroSeconds` defaults to three seconds and should match the approved intro duration.

## 5. Test before calling audio finished

After rebuilding/reopening the place:

1. Check Output for asset-permission/loading failures. Start once with all slots empty: subtitles must work and no voice/music should be claimed or heard.
2. Add one valid number recording and the lobby track. Confirm the track loops through intermission/drawing/results and that the recording's spoken number matches both HUD and world display.
3. Fill all 60 numbers and test a full round with two clients, including a late join and simulated network delay. A late client must never replay the history of spoken numbers.
4. Listen for narration overlap, truncation, long silence or wrong-number recordings. Verify declared durations and subtitles against the actual files.
5. Speak during music: it should duck and recover. Adjust music while voice plays; mute voice and confirm music returns to its selected level. Test each channel's mute independently and rejoin to verify settings save.
6. Test track switching with valid bonus tracks in a developer audio test; full gameplay triggering is still pending the bonus rules. Do not report the bonus soundtracks as gameplay-complete until that integration exists.

Mock tests verify queueing, ducking and volume logic. They cannot listen to recordings, validate the license, verify Roblox asset permissions or certify real-device timing.
